# Forza Horizon 6 — Locations: Tokyo City and Landmarks

Tokyo City and its districts, plus the documented landmarks and points of
interest around Japan.

=== Locations ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
What are the main locations in Forza Horizon 6? The world is a fictionalised
Japan containing Tokyo City (the largest urban area ever in a Forza Horizon
game), rural countryside, alpine regions, coasts and mountain villages.
Documented landmarks and points of interest include Nachi Falls, Ruriko-ji
Temple, Hirosaki Castle, Ine Fishing Village, Shirakawa-go, Mount Haruna,
Tateyama Kurobe, the Urakawa Space Center and Mount Fuji, along with the
festival sites — the Horizon Festival Japan Site and the Legend Island
circuit. The Journal's fog-of-war map tracks which of these you have
discovered.

=== Tokyo City ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
Where is Tokyo City? Tokyo City is the largest urban area in a Forza
Horizon game — described as five times larger than the urban areas of
previous titles and the most complex and intricate drivable space in the
game. It sits on the Japan map as the home for car enthusiasts, running
from pleasant suburban outskirts and neon-soaked downtown streets to the
docks and industrial districts, with elevated highways threading through
it. It is also the setting for the game's introduction races, and the
island of Legend sits directly east of its docks (Tokyo Docks).

=== Tokyo City Districts ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
What districts are in Tokyo City? Documented districts and landmarks:
- Shibuya Crossing — the famous crossing, close to Ginkgo Avenue; a
  documented drift-spot challenge asks for drift skills here, and a Speed
  Trap event is set at 160 mph for A class.
- Ginkgo Avenue — a golden-hued pocket of nature in the city where Shibuya
  Crossing sits a short drive away.
- Akihabara — a buzzing retail hub for technology and entertainment,
  constantly alive and electric.
- Daikoku — a chilled-out spot a short distance away, tied to Japanese car
  meet culture.
- The docks and industrial districts — the tougher, working side of the
  city, used for street races like Tokyo City Docks Charge.
- Tokyo Tower — a documented photo-challenge landmark at its base.
- Edogawa area — includes the Edogawa Baseball Stadium (photo challenge)
  and Edogawa Cross Country routes.
The design brief for the city was many "little vibes": looming skyscrapers
and lightshows mixed with small green spaces, all highly layered and
three-dimensional.

=== Landmarks ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
What landmarks can I visit? Documented landmarks, all rebuilt with input
from cultural consultant Kyoko Yamashita:
- Nachi Falls — Japan's tallest waterfall with a 133 metre drop, backdrop to
  the Kumano Nachi Taisha Grand Shrine; you can drive right up to it, an XP
  bonus board is hidden behind the falls, and an Aftermarket Car waits
  downstream.
- Ruriko-ji Temple — a Buddhist temple recognised by its towering
  five-tiered pagoda surrounded by greenery.
- Hirosaki Castle — a black-and-white castle framed by sakura (cherry
  blossom) trees; notably, its cherry blossoms are among the only trees you
  cannot smash, out of respect.
- Ine Fishing Village — a charming coastal town whose Funaya boathouses
  line Ine Bay; there is a player house for sale in Ine and a story mission
  nearby.
- Shirakawa-go — a village of thatched farmhouses deep in the Ryohaku
  Mountains, with heavy seasonal snowfall.
- Urakawa Space Center — a modern space facility you race to during the
  game's introduction, complete with a Drag Meet and race routes.
- Mount Haruna — thin, winding cliff roads requiring extreme control, with
  miles of landscape visible from the top.
- Tateyama Kurobe — an epic alpine route snaking between towering blocks of
  snow.

=== Points of Interest and Secrets ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
How do I find things to do in the world? Points of interest are tracked in
your Journal as a fog-of-war map fills in. Photographing landmarks, murals
and points of interest earns Stamps. Secrets are placed deliberately: an XP
bonus board hides behind Nachi Falls, Aftermarket Cars are parked at fixed
spots around the map (near drag strips, city events and landmarks), Barn
Find Rumors unlock as you collect Stamps, and murals by real artists
(Kenji Iwasaki, Dragon76, Lady Aiko and others) are scattered for you to
photograph.
