# Forza Horizon 6 — Tuning

How tuning works in Forza Horizon 6: which upgrades unlock which sliders,
what each tuning setting does, and how to tune step by step.

=== Tuning ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Tuning
What is tuning? Tuning is the process of adjusting the settings your
installed upgrade parts have unlocked — tire pressure, gearing, alignment,
springs, damping, anti-roll bars, brakes and more — to make a specific car
behave the way you want for a specific discipline. Tuning in Forza Horizon 6
is as deep as it is rewarding: parts decide which sliders you are allowed to
move, and the sliders decide how the car responds to your inputs, how it
handles weight transfer through corners, and how predictable it feels at the
limit.

=== How Do I Tune My Car ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Tuning
How do I tune my car? Follow this flow:
1. Open the Upgrade Shop and install parts that unlock the sliders you need.
   The single biggest unlock is Race-tier Springs & Dampers, which opens the
   full suspension tuning suite; Race anti-roll bars and Race brakes unlock
   their own sliders.
2. Open the Tune menu for that car.
3. Adjust tire pressure and gearing first, then alignment (camber, toe,
   caster), spring rates, ride height, damping (bump and rebound), anti-roll
   bar stiffness, brake balance and brake pressure, and the differential if
   available.
4. Test the car on a lap or a short event, then adjust based on feel:
   if the car understeers, soften the front; if it oversteers, soften the
   rear.
5. Save the tune — you can also share tunes, and download other players'
   tunes at Car Meets and from the community.
You can tune any car you own; stock cars with no upgraded parts have no
sliders available to adjust.

=== Tuning Settings ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Tuning
What settings can I tune? The documented tuning settings in Forza Horizon 6
are:
- Tire pressure — lower pressure generally gives more grip, higher pressure
  faster steering response; the street suspension tiers are what usually
  unlock this slider first.
- Gearing — final drive and individual gear ratios for acceleration or top
  speed.
- Alignment — front and rear camber, front and rear toe, and front caster.
  Negative camber adds cornering grip at the cost of straight-line grip;
  slight toe-out sharpens turn-in; toe-in adds high-speed stability.
- Spring rates (front and rear) and ride height (front and rear).
- Damping — bump (compression) and rebound (extension) for front and rear;
  rebound is normally stiffer than bump.
- Anti-roll bar stiffness front and rear (roughly 1.00 soft to 65.00 stiff).
- Brake balance (front-to-rear split) and brake pressure.
- Differential settings where the car supports them.
Each slider is only available if the corresponding upgraded part is fitted.

=== Suspension Upgrades and Tuning Unlocks ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Tuning
Which suspension upgrades unlock tuning? Documented tiers:
- Stock Springs & Dampers: factory suspension, no tuning sliders unlocked.
- Street Springs & Dampers: usually unlocks the tire pressure slider but
  keeps alignment, springs, damping and ride height locked.
- Sport Springs & Dampers: modest improvement, typically still locked
  sliders; saves Performance Index compared with Race.
- Race Springs & Dampers: unlocks the full suspension tuning suite —
  alignment (camber, toe, caster), spring rate front and rear, ride height
  front and rear, and damping (bump and rebound) front and rear. This is
  the standard choice for any serious build at A class and above.
- Rally Springs & Dampers: for chassis that support off-road conversions;
  full suite calibrated for loose surfaces.
- Off-Road Springs & Dampers: maximum compliance, values tuned for extreme
  terrain and cross-country builds.
Race anti-roll bars unlock the ARB stiffness slider for that axle, and Race
brakes unlock brake balance and brake pressure.

=== Tuning Tips ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Tuning
What are good tuning tips for beginners? Documented guidance:
- If the car understeers, soften the front anti-roll bar; don't stiffen the
  rear to fix understeer. If it oversteers, soften the rear.
- Don't max anti-roll bar stiffness on both ends — very stiff bars make the
  car skip over road texture and lose grip; a middle range is typical.
- On road racing builds, lower ride height for a lower centre of gravity;
  raise it for dirt and cross-country so you can clear terrain.
- Front-wheel-drive cars want more front brake bias than rear-wheel-drive
  or all-wheel-drive cars.
- Leave toe at 0.0 unless you need a specific fix — any non-zero toe causes
  tire scrub.
- Weight reduction is one of the few upgrades that simultaneously improves
  acceleration, braking and cornering; on very light cars check whether the
  Performance Index cost is worth it.
- Drag builds skip chassis reinforcement — the added weight hurts the
  launch.
- Drift setups typically use aggressive front camber, a soft front and very
  stiff rear anti-roll bar, and a more rearward brake bias.

=== Share and Download Tunes ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Tuning
Can I use other players' tunes? Yes. Tunes can be shared with the
community, and at Car Meets you can download other players' Tunes, Liveries
and even full copies of their cars. Festival Playlist guides and community
resources regularly point at shared tunes for seasonal events, so downloading
a proven tune is a normal alternative to tuning everything yourself.
