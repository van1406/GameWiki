# Forza Horizon 6 — The Japan Map: Biomes, Regions and Roads

How the open world is laid out: the biggest Horizon map yet, its biomes,
regions and signature roads.

=== Japan Map ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
How big is the Forza Horizon 6 map? The Japan map is the franchise's
largest open world, the most dense map the series has made, full of
verticality, diverse biomes and spectacular driving experiences. It is a
fictionalised version of Japan — not a one-to-one recreation — with Mount
Fuji and Tokyo confirmed, connecting real-world landmarks in an arrangement
that works for driving. Tokyo City is its biggest urban area, and the rest
of the map runs through countryside, alpine hills, coasts and mountains.
The world changes with weekly seasons, and a fog-of-war Journal map
remembers where you have been.

=== Biomes ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
What biomes are in Forza Horizon 6? The documented biomes of Japan include
alpine region, coast, highlands, lowlands, plains, Tokyo (the urban biome)
and snow country. The transition between biomes is used to create scale —
the linear distance may not be massive, but watching the scenery change as
you move sells the size of the world. Notable biome-flavoured spots:
snow-covered alpine hills, the forests around Shirakawa-go, the coastal
town of Ine, and the neon density of Tokyo City. Seasonal changes (a week
of spring, then summer, autumn and winter) shift tone, activity and sound,
and events like the Shirakawa Circuit look and play completely differently
depending on the season.

=== Major Roads ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
What are the signature roads? Documented major roads and routes:
- The Highway Loop — the high-speed ring road used by The Colossus race,
  where you cruise above 200 mph for most of the way; Tokyo's elevated
  highways were built with the team's Hot Wheels expansion experience.
- Mount Haruna — thin, winding mountain roads down the cliffs that demand
  extreme control; a classic touge setting.
- Tateyama Kurobe — a snowy alpine route through towering snow walls.
- Touge mountain passes — night hillside courses used by Touge Battles and
  Time Attack circuits like the Hokobu Time Circuit in the mountain valleys.
- Coastal and countryside roads linking villages such as Ine and regions
  across the map, plus the urban grid of Tokyo City with its suburbs,
  downtown streets and docks.
Roads are also tracked in your Journal — the game counts discovered roads
as part of exploration progress.

=== Regions ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
What regions are on the map? Event routes and challenges document regions
and areas across Japan, including the Tokyo City region, Hokubu (home of
the Hokubu Time Circuit and Hokubu Trail), Takashiro, Sotoyama, Ohtani,
Nangan, Minamino, Soni, Narai-Juku, Ine/Kita Ine on the coast, and the
alpine areas around Mount Haruna and Tateyama Kurobe. Each region blends
into a biome — snow, forest, coast or city — and hosts its own circuits,
sprints, trails, cross-country routes, treasure hunts and PR stunt targets.

=== Seasons ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Locations
How do seasons work? Forza Horizon 6 runs dynamic seasons in weekly
installments — a week of spring, a week of summer, and so on. Seasons
genuinely inform the world: they shift tone, activity and sound, change
weather and surface conditions, and alter how events play (the same circuit
races differently in summer than in winter). They also drive the Festival
Playlist, which rotates with the season, and reset Time Attack leaderboards
when they change. Cherry blossom blooms in sakura season and heavy snowfall
in winter are called out as signature seasonal moments, especially around
Shirakawa-go.
