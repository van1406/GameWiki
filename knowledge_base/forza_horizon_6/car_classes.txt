# Forza Horizon 6 — Car Classes and Performance

The Performance Index, the class ladder including the new R class, how
Wristbands gate class restrictions, and the difference between stock and
tuned performance.

=== Car Classes ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Cars
How do car classes work? Every car in Forza Horizon 6 is placed in a
performance class based on its Performance Index: D, C, B, A, S1 and S2 for
road-going machinery, plus the new R class introduced in Forza Horizon 6
for track-focused cars. Races, Championships, Time Attacks and Drag Meets
all specify a class restriction (for example "A Class" or "S2"), and you
must bring a car at or below that class. Your Wristband level determines
which class restrictions you get invited to: higher Wristbands open faster,
higher-class events.

=== Performance Index ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Cars
What is the Performance Index? The Performance Index (PI) is the numeric
rating that decides which class a car falls into. For Forza Horizon 6 the
Performance Index was overhauled to ensure cars are more balanced within
each performance class, and the overhaul introduced the new R class for
track-focused cars. Any part you install costs PI, so upgrading a car can
push it into the next class — builds are therefore about spending PI budget
where it matters most (tires, power or chassis) rather than maxing
everything out.

=== Class Restrictions ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Cars
Where do class restrictions apply? Events in Forza Horizon 6 are tied to
car classes: official festival races, Festival Playlist Championships (such
as "Retro Hot Hatch - C Class" or "Modern Supercars - S1 Class"), Time
Attack Circuits, Drag Meets and PR Stunts all publish a class and often a
target. Your Wristband controls the invites you receive — as you earn new
Wristbands you participate in races with higher car class restrictions. If
you have already beaten an event once, the Race Customizer lets you replay
it with any car in your garage.

=== Stock vs Tuned Performance ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Cars
What is the difference between stock and tuned performance? Stock
performance is how the car behaves straight from the Autoshow or a reward,
with factory parts, factory suspension and factory tuning sliders — its
published stats (Speed, Handling, Acceleration, Launch, Braking, Offroad)
describe that state. Tuned performance is what the same car can do after
upgrades and tuning: new tires, suspension, brakes, weight reduction, engine
swaps and slider tuning can transform a car, but tuning also changes its
Performance Index and can move it into a higher class. When a car is
recommended for a task, check whether the recommendation is about its stock
state (good out of the box) or about its tuned potential (strong once built
and tuned), because the two can be very different. For example, the 1985
Toyota Sprinter Trueno (AE86) Forza Edition has modest stock stats but
exceeds 322 mph when fully upgraded with an engine swap, all-wheel-drive
conversion and a specialised drag tune.

=== Drivetrain ===
Game: Forza Horizon 6
Category: Cars
What drivetrains are available? Cars in Forza Horizon 6 come in front-wheel
drive, rear-wheel drive and all-wheel drive configurations, and drivetrain
conversion is one of the upgrade options — for instance the AE86 drag build
mentioned above uses an all-wheel-drive conversion. Drivetrain choice
changes how the car behaves: AWD cars generally launch harder and are more
forgiving on loose surfaces, while RWD cars reward throttle control and are
favourite platforms for drifting. Drivetrain is also a matchmaking filter in
Horizon Play's Custom Drifting, where you can choose the car class and drive
type.
