# Minecraft — Villagers and Trading

How villagers work, every profession and workstation, how to get villagers,
and trading strategies that turn villages into your best source of gear.

=== How Villagers Work ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Villagers
Villagers are passive NPCs that live in villages, take professions from
workstation blocks, trade with players, breed when there is enough food and
space, and are defended by iron golems. Each villager keeps an inventory of
trades tied to its profession and level. Villagers hide indoors at night,
ring bells when raids come, and remember the players who trade with them.
Killing villagers angers the village (and its golem) and is never worth it —
their trades are far more valuable than their drops.

=== Professions and Workstations ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Villagers
A villager becomes a professional when it links to an unused workstation
block. The professions and their job sites are:
- Librarian → lectern (sells enchanted books, including Mending)
- Armorer → blast furnace (iron, chainmail, diamond armor)
- Weaponsmith → grindstone (swords and axes)
- Toolsmith → smithing table (pickaxes, shovels, axes)
- Butcher → smoker (cooked meat trades)
- Cartographer → cartography table (explorer maps, banners, paper trades)
- Cleric → brewing stand (redstone, lapis, glowstone, ender pearls)
- Farmer → composter (crops for emeralds, golden carrots)
- Fisherman → barrel (fish, strings, cooked cod trades)
- Fletcher → fletching table (arrows, bows, sticks-for-emeralds)
- Leatherworker → cauldron (leather armor, horse armor, saddles)
- Mason → stonecutter (quartz, terracotta, bricks, decorative blocks)
- Shepherd → loom (banners, wool trades)
- Nitwit: villagers with no profession block cannot work or trade.
Breaking the workstation unlinks the profession until the villager links
again — this is how trade lists can be rerolled before locking them.

=== Getting Villagers ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Villagers
How do I get villagers?
1. Find a village — they generate in plains, savannas, deserts, taigas, snowy
   plains, and meadows.
2. Breed them: provide extra beds and food (villagers pick up bread, carrots,
   potatoes, or beetroot and become willing); babies grow up quickly.
3. Move them safely: boats, minecarts, and rails carry villagers where you
   want them — build a walled trading hall with a bed and workstation for
   each one.
4. Cure zombie villagers: trap one, throw a splash potion of weakness on it,
   and feed it a golden apple; it shakes, then converts back into a normal
   villager. Cured villagers give you heavily discounted trades.
Tips: Keep villagers fed and housed — no beds means no breeding, and a
villager without a bed and workstation will not settle into a profession.

=== Trading Basics ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Villagers
How does villager trading work? Villagers trade items for emeralds and back.
Each villager starts at novice level with two trades and unlocks more as you
trade with it — novice, apprentice, journeyman, expert, master. A trade slot
locks after several uses and restocks (the villager goes to work at its
workstation) up to twice a day. Emeralds are the currency: farmers buy crops,
clerics buy rotten flesh, and librarians sell enchanted books for emeralds.
Trades once used are locked in, so choose the trades you level up carefully.

=== Important Trades ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Villagers
Which trades are the most valuable?
- Librarian: enchanted books — Mending, Unbreaking, Protection, Fortune — are
  the most sought-after trades in the game.
- Armorer and toolsmith: diamond gear at the master level.
- Fletcher: sticks for emeralds early, then arrows and bows.
- Farmer: bulk crops for emeralds and golden carrots for food.
- Cleric: ender pearls, redstone, lapis, and glowstone for emeralds.
- Cartographer: woodland and ocean explorer maps that lead to mansions and
  monuments.
Tips: A fletcher and a farmer are the easiest first emerald engines — sticks
and crops are cheap, and both restock twice a day.

=== Trading Strategies ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Villagers
How do I get the best villager trades?
1. Build a trading hall: one cell per villager with a bed and its workstation,
   well lit and walled so no golem or zombie gets in.
2. Reroll librarians: before trading with a librarian, break and replace the
   lectern until the book you want appears, then trade once to lock it.
3. Cure zombie villagers for big discounts — prices drop dramatically for
   cured villagers.
4. Win a raid for the Hero of the Village effect, which gives further price
   cuts and village gifts.
5. Breed a second generation inside your hall so you never run out.
6. Keep a farmer planting and a composter handy so your emerald supply never
   stops.
Tips: Never hit a villager or a golem in the hall — iron golems defend
villagers and prices rise with bad reputation.

=== Villagers and Raids ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Villagers
Raids tie directly into villager economics: killing a raid captain grants Bad
Omen, and entering a village with Bad Omen starts a raid. Defeating the raid
grants Hero of the Village, which both discounts villager trades and causes
villagers to toss gifts (emeralds, food, and more) at you. Clear pillager
captains near villages carefully — a raid you win pays for itself in cheaper
diamond gear.

=== Iron Golems and Village Defense ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Villagers
Each village spawns iron golems that protect villagers from zombies,
skeletons, and pillagers. You can build your own golem from four iron blocks
in a T shape with a carved pumpkin on top to defend your trading hall. Golems
patrol, launch hostile mobs into the air, and ignore you unless you hurt
villagers — keep their village peaceful and they stay on your side.
