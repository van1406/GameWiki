# Minecraft — Progression Guides

Step-by-step how-to guides for Minecraft's main progression: first night, diamonds,
the Nether, and the End.

=== Surviving the First Night ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How do I survive the first night? 
1. Punch a tree to get wood logs, craft planks, then a crafting table and a wooden
   pickaxe.
2. Mine stone to upgrade to stone tools, and dig coal for torches.
3. Find sheep for wool and craft a bed (3 wool + 3 planks), or gather enough coal
   for torches.
4. Before sunset, either build a small closed shelter with a door or dig into a
   hillside and seal the entrance; light the inside with torches.
5. Sleep in the bed to skip the night, or mine safely indoors until morning.
Tips: Hostile mobs spawn in the dark, so light everything; never wander at night
without a sword and food.

=== Finding Diamonds ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How do I find diamonds? Mine down to below Y level 16 — diamonds are most common
between Y -58 and Y -53, just above the bedrock layer. Bring an iron pickaxe or
better (diamond ore drops nothing without one), plenty of torches, and a water
bucket for lava. The classic method is branch mining: dig a main tunnel and carve
short side branches every few blocks at the deepest safe level. Watch for lava
pockets at the bottom of the world. Fortune III on a pickaxe multiplies diamond
drops once you reach enchanting.

=== Entering the Nether ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How do I make a Nether portal / enter the Nether? Mine at least 10 obsidian blocks
(a diamond pickaxe is required to mine obsidian) with a lava source and water
bucket, or find a ruined portal. Build a rectangular frame 4 blocks tall and 5
wide (corners optional), then light the inside with flint and steel to open the
purple portal. Step into it to enter the Nether. Bring gold armor, food, a sword,
and fire resistance potions — the Nether is hostile.

=== Finding a Nether Fortress ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How do I find a Nether fortress? Fortresses are dark structures of nether brick
found anywhere in the Nether. From your portal, bridge out across open areas and
watch for large bridges and towers; exploring in a straight line covers more
ground. Bring fire resistance potions, a shield, and lots of blocks for bridging
over lava. Fortresses contain blaze spawners (needed for blaze rods), nether wart,
and wither skeletons — mark your route with torches so you can find the way back.

=== Reaching the End ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How do I reach the End? Craft eyes of ender from ender pearls (dropped by
endermen) and blaze powder (from blaze rods). Throw an eye of ender and follow it
— it floats toward the buried Stronghold. Dig down to the portal room, place an
eye of ender in each of the twelve portal frame slots, and jump into the activated
portal. Prepare first: full diamond armor, a good sword and bow, plenty of food,
building blocks, and a bed or water bucket for the dragon fight.

=== Defeating the Ender Dragon ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How do I defeat the Ender Dragon? The fight has two phases:
1. Destroy the end crystals on top of the obsidian pillars — shoot them with a bow
   or pillar up with blocks and melee them (some are covered by iron cages; climb
   and hit them through the opening). Crystals heal the dragon, so they must all
   go down first.
2. Attack the dragon whenever it perches on the exit portal: rush in with a
   strength or sharpness-enchanted sword, land several hits, and back away before
   it takes off and knocks you back.
Tips: Bring a bow with many arrows, blocks, food, and a water bucket to clear
dragon breath (it damages over time — collect it with glass bottles instead).
Beds explode in the End and experienced players use that explosion damage against
the perched dragon, at the cost of the bed. Killing the dragon grants 12,000
experience points and spawns the dragon egg on the exit portal.

=== Enchanting ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How does enchanting work? Craft an enchanting table from 2 diamonds, 4 obsidian,
and 1 book, then place it with 15 bookshelves arranged one block apart around it
to reach level 30 enchants. Enchanting spends experience levels plus lapis lazuli:
put the item and lapis in the table, choose an offered enchantment, and the item
gains it. Useful enchantments include Efficiency and Fortune for mining,
Unbreaking and Mending for durability, Sharpness and Looting for weapons, and
Protection for armor. Combine enchantments on an anvil with enchanted books, and
use a grindstone to disenchant items for experience back.

=== Villager Trading ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How does villager trading work? Villagers trade based on their profession, which
they take from a nearby workstation block — a composter makes a farmer, a lectern
a librarian, a blast furnace an armorer, a brewing stand a cleric, a cartography
table a cartographer, and so on. Trade items for emeralds and back; each villager
has a limited set of trades that restocks twice a day when the villager works at
its job site. Trading repeatedly levels the villager up (novice to master),
unlocking better trades and discounts. Librarians can sell enchanted books, making
them the most valuable profession for gear.

=== Farming ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How does farming work? Till dirt or grass blocks with a hoe to create farmland and
keep it within four blocks of water so it stays hydrated. Plant wheat from seeds,
carrots, potatoes, beetroot, and nether wart (in the Nether on soul sand). Crops
grow over time — bone meal instantly fertilizes them. Animals can be farmed by
penning two of the same animal and feeding them their favorite food (wheat for
cows and sheep, carrots for pigs, seeds for chickens) to breed them. Automatic
farms use water streams, dispensers, and redstone timers to harvest crops.

=== Redstone ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Progression
How does redstone work? Redstone dust carries a power signal up to 15 blocks from a
power source such as a lever, button, redstone torch, or pressure plate. Repeaters
extend the signal and add delays; comparators read container fullness and compare
signals; pistons push blocks; and dispensers fire items. These parts build automatic
doors, farms, lighting systems, and traps. Start simple — a lever wired to a door —
then add repeaters and torch logic for more complex machines.
