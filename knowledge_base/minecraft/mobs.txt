# Minecraft — Mobs

A field guide to the creatures (mobs) found in Minecraft. Each mob lists what it
is, where it spawns, its behavior, drops, how to fight it, and useful tips.

=== Zombie ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A hostile undead mob with 20 health points (10 hearts).
Where it spawns: In dark places — caves, unlit buildings, and shaded surfaces at
night; they burn in direct sunlight.
Behavior: Walks toward the player and attacks in melee; baby zombies are smaller,
faster, and harder to hit. Zombies can break wooden doors on hard difficulty.
Drops: Rotten flesh, with occasional rare drops of iron ingots, carrots, or
potatoes.
How to fight it: A sword kills them in a few hits; a shield blocks their melee
attacks, and sunlight will finish off surface zombies on its own.
Useful tips: Light up your base with torches so they never spawn inside; keep a
shield up when fighting groups.

=== Skeleton ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A hostile undead archer with 20 health points (10 hearts).
Where it spawns: In dark areas like caves and unlit surfaces at night; they burn
in sunlight. The stray is a cold variant found in icy biomes.
Behavior: Shoots arrows from a distance and backs away from the player to keep its
range.
Drops: Bones and arrows.
How to fight it: Hold up a shield to block arrows, then close the distance and
finish it with a sword; a bow works well from cover.
Useful tips: Bones can be used to tame wolves and craft bone meal; strafing side to
side makes their shots miss.

=== Creeper ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A silent green hostile mob that explodes.
Where it spawns: In dark areas — caves and unlit surfaces at night; it does not
burn in sunlight.
Behavior: Walks toward the player silently, then lights up with a short hissing
fuse before exploding. The explosion damages blocks and the player.
Drops: Gunpowder, used to craft TNT and fireworks.
How to fight it: Strike it with a sword and immediately back away before the fuse
finishes, repeating until it dies; a bow kills it from a safe distance. Never
fight one next to your build.
Useful tips: Armor does not reduce explosion damage much, so distance is the best
defense; keep your base well lit so creepers cannot spawn inside.

=== Enderman ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A tall, neutral black mob with 40 health points (20 hearts) that
teleports.
Where it spawns: In dark areas of the Overworld, the Nether, and the End.
Behavior: Wanders and picks up blocks; becomes hostile if you look directly at its
face or attack it. It takes damage from water.
Drops: Ender pearls, used to craft eyes of ender to find the Stronghold.
How to fight it: Look at its feet to stay safe, then strike its legs with a sword;
water (a splash of rain or a bucket) hurts it and stops it teleporting.
Useful tips: Wearing a pumpkin on your head keeps endermen neutral when you look
at them; never make eye contact in the End without armor.

=== Spider ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A climbing hostile/neutral mob with 16 health points (8 hearts).
Where it spawns: In dark areas; in daylight it is neutral, in low light it turns
hostile.
Behavior: Climbs walls and jumps at the player; aggressive only in darkness or
when attacked.
Drops: String and spider eyes (string for bows and fishing rods, spider eyes for
poison potions).
How to fight it: A sword handles them quickly; keep a light source nearby so they
stay neutral during the day.
Useful tips: Block off cave entrances so spiders cannot climb into your base.

=== Villager ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A passive humanoid that lives in villages and trades with the player.
Where it spawns: In villages across the Overworld, taking jobs from workstation
blocks (composters, lecterns, blast furnaces, and more).
Behavior: Wanders the village, works at its job site, hides indoors at night, and
breeds when there are enough beds and food.
Drops: Nothing — killing villagers is punished by the village iron golems.
How to fight it: You should not fight them — trade instead.
Useful tips: Trade with villagers to buy and sell items for emeralds; different
professions (farmer, librarian, armorer, cleric) offer different trades.

=== Iron Golem ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A large defensive mob with 100 health points (50 hearts) that protects
villagers.
Where it spawns: Naturally in villages, or built from four iron blocks in a T
shape with a carved pumpkin on top.
Behavior: Patrols the village, throws hostile mobs into the air, and does not take
knockback; it attacks players who repeatedly harm villagers.
Drops: Iron ingots and a poppy when killed.
How to fight it: Only if you must — use a sword and keep moving, since its hits
launch you; attacking villagers first will make it turn on you.
Useful tips: Build your own golem to defend a base; it is the best early defense
against creepers and zombies.

=== Wolf ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A tameable neutral mob with 8 health points (4 hearts), gaining 3
more hearts after taming.
Where it spawns: In forest and taiga biomes in packs.
Behavior: Neutral until attacked; can be tamed with bones, after which it follows
the player, sits on command, and fights mobs that attack its owner.
Drops: Nothing of value.
How to fight it: Avoid packs — a whole pack attacks if you hit one; individual
wolves fall to a sword quickly.
Useful tips: Tame several wolves with bones, feed them meat to heal them, and they
become a traveling guard dog squad.

=== Cow, Pig, and Chicken ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: Passive farm animals.
Where it spawns: On grassy surfaces in the Overworld, or bred in animal farms.
Behavior: Wander aimlessly; can be bred with wheat (cows), carrots or potatoes
(pigs), and seeds (chickens).
Drops: Cows drop leather and raw beef, pigs drop raw porkchops, and chickens drop
raw chicken and feathers.
How to fight it: One punch or sword hit is usually enough — they do not fight back.
Useful tips: Breed animals for a renewable food and leather supply; chickens drop
feathers needed for arrows.

=== Slime ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A green hostile blob that splits when killed; health varies by size.
Where it spawns: In swamps at night and in specific deep underground chunks.
Behavior: Hops toward the player and deals damage on contact; larger slimes split
into smaller ones when killed.
Drops: Slimeballs from the smallest slimes, used for sticky pistons and leads.
How to fight it: Kill the big one, then clear the small ones — only the smallest
slimes drop loot and they barely damage you.
Useful tips: Slimeballs are essential for sticky pistons in redstone builds.

=== Blaze ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A fiery flying mob of the Nether that drops blaze rods.
Where it spawns: In Nether fortresses, spawned from blaze spawners and naturally
near fortress platforms.
Behavior: Hovers and shoots volleys of fireballs, diving down for melee attacks
that set the player on fire; often appears in groups.
Drops: Blaze rods, the material for blaze powder, eyes of ender, and brewing fuel.
How to fight it: Take cover from the fire volleys, then rush in with a sword
between shots; a bow works from range. Snowballs and water also damage blazes.
Useful tips: Bring a shield and fire resistance potions — blazes are the most
dangerous regular Nether mob and blaze rods are mandatory for reaching the End.

=== The Wither ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A hostile boss mob with 300 health points (150 hearts) on normal
difficulty.
Where it spawns: Summoned by the player — four soul sand or soul soil in a T shape
with three wither skeleton skulls on top.
Behavior: Shoots explosive skulls, applies the Wither effect that drains health,
and destroys blocks around it.
Drops: A nether star, used to craft a beacon.
How to fight it: Summon it in an open area or tunnel, wear full protection armor,
use a bow to chip its health, and finish it with a strength-enchanted sword.
Useful tips: The wither effect ignores armor — bring regeneration potions and
golden apples; fighting it underground limits its ability to fly away.

=== The Ender Dragon ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: The final boss of Minecraft with 200 health points (100 hearts),
found in the End dimension.
Where it spawns: The End dimension, reached through a fully lit End portal in the
Stronghold.
Behavior: Flies around the central pillars healing from end crystals on top of
them, swoops at the player, and breathes dragon breath that damages over time.
Drops: 12,000 experience points and, on the first kill, a dragon egg appears on
the exit portal.
How to fight it: First destroy the end crystals on the obsidian pillars (shoot
them or pillar up and melee the caged ones), then attack the dragon with a sword
whenever it perches on the portal, and retreat between its charges.
Useful tips: Bring a bow with plenty of arrows, blocks to pillar up with, water
buckets to clear dragon breath, and food — the fight is a marathon, not a sprint.

=== Drowned ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A hostile undead zombie that spawns underwater.
Where it spawns: In oceans, rivers, and flooded caves at night; they also
rise from water on land at night. Some drowned spawn already holding a
trident.
Behavior: Walks slowly on land but swims quickly toward you in water; a
trident-carrying drowned throws its trident from range.
Drops: Rotten flesh, sometimes copper ingots, and nautilus shells; a drowned
that drops its trident gives you one of the rarest weapons in the game.
How to fight it: Fight them on land where you have the advantage, block
trident throws with a shield, and finish them with a sword.
Useful tips: Keep your harbor and canal walls tall — drowned climb out of
the water near your base. Water breathing potions make ocean fights fair.

=== Witch ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A hostile potion-throwing mob with 26 health points (13 hearts).
Where it spawns: In dark areas at night like other monsters, and naturally in
swamp huts.
Behavior: Drinks potions to heal itself or gain fire resistance, then throws
splash potions of harming, poison, and slowness at the player from range.
Drops: Glass bottles, sugar, redstone, glowstone dust, sticks, spider eyes,
and gunpowder — a mini brewing-kit loot table.
How to fight it: Rush it with a sword before it can drink, or use a bow;
strafing makes its splash potions miss. Kill it quickly — poison witches are
the dangerous ones.
Useful tips: Swamp huts are free witch loot; witches also drop brewing
ingredients, so their drops stock your potion chest.

=== Phantom ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A hostile flying undead mob that burns away at sunrise.
Where it spawns: In the sky at night when a player has not slept for three
or more in-game days (the insomnia mechanic).
Behavior: Circles above the player, then swoops down in dive attacks,
screeching as it lines up each pass.
Drops: Phantom membrane — used to repair elytra and to brew potions of slow
falling.
How to fight it: Hit it as it dives past (a sword swing mid-air works), or
use a bow; it cannot attack while circling.
Useful tips: Sleep in a bed every night and phantoms never spawn. If you
need membranes, stay awake deliberately and fight them above a lit roof.

=== Ghast ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A giant floating hostile mob of the Nether with 10 health points
(5 hearts) but devastating fireball attacks.
Where it spawns: In the open caverns of the Nether, especially over lava
seas.
Behavior: Drifts aimlessly, screams, and fires explosive fireballs at the
player when it has a line of sight.
Drops: Ghast tears (used for regeneration potions and end crystals) and
gunpowder.
How to fight it: Deflect its fireball back with a well-timed sword swing,
or shoot it with a bow between volleys; fire resistance removes the risk of
its attacks entirely.
Useful tips: Build your Nether portal with a roof and keep blocks around it —
a ghast can destroy unprotected builds and fireballs knock you into lava.

=== Zombified Piglin ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A neutral undead Nether mob (the zombified form of the piglin).
Where it spawns: Throughout the Nether wastes and near nether gold ore, in
groups of up to several at a time.
Behavior: Neutral until one is attacked — then every zombified piglin in
range swarms the attacker, and they do not burn in sunlight (they are
Nether-native).
Drops: Rotten flesh and gold nuggets, with a rare chance of a gold ingot.
How to fight it: Attack from cover and keep backing away — they have no
ranged attacks and pile up quickly in the open.
Useful tips: They are a great gold-nugget farm if you have a safe enclosed
arena; lava or fall traps handle packs that follow you through a portal.

=== Piglin ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A neutral (unless provoked) humanoid Nether mob that loves gold.
Where it spawns: In nether wastes and bastion remnants, usually in groups.
Behavior: Attacks anyone who is not wearing at least one piece of gold
armor; piglins get angry if you mine gold in front of them or open their
chests. Throw a gold ingot at one and it barters: it examines the ingot and
tosses back a random item (ender pearls, obsidian, string, arrows, and
more).
Drops: Rotten flesh when killed.
How to fight it: Wear gold armor to stay peaceful, or fight with a sword —
they come in packs, so keep moving.
Useful tips: Bartering is the only renewable source of some Nether items;
barter with a stack of gold ingots in a safe room for ender pearls before the
End.

=== Hoglin ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A large hostile boar-like beast of the Nether.
Where it spawns: Crimson forests in small groups.
Behavior: Charges and flings the player with its tusks; it flees briefly after
taking damage and is repelled by crimson fungi and warped fungus.
Drops: Raw porkchops and leather — real food and crafting material from the
Nether.
How to fight it: Hit and back away while it reels from damage; warped fungus
placed around your build keeps them out.
Useful tips: A crimson forest hoglin pen is a renewable food source in the
Nether — funnel them into a pen with warped trapdoors they will not cross.

=== Wither Skeleton ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A tall, black undead skeleton that attacks with a stone sword and
the Wither effect.
Where it spawns: In Nether fortresses, on the dark nether-brick corridors and
bridges.
Behavior: Sneaks toward the player and strikes in melee; a successful hit
applies Wither, which turns your hearts black and drains health over time.
Drops: Coal, bones, and rarely a wither skeleton skull — three skulls plus
soul sand summon the Wither boss.
How to fight it: Fight on open ground where you can circle, use a sword with
Knockback to keep it at range, and drink milk or regeneration potions to
clear the Wither effect.
Useful tips: Block fortress corridors to one-block gaps so only one skeleton
faces you at a time; skulls are the long-term goal of every fortress run.

=== Guardian ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A hostile fish-like monument guard with spines and a laser
attack.
Where it spawns: In and around ocean monuments.
Behavior: Sits at range, charges a bright laser beam, then fires it — the
beam grows stronger the longer it is locked on. Its spines damage anything
that melee-hits it, and it flops toward players who get close on land.
Drops: Prismarine shards, prismarine crystals, and raw cod.
How to fight it: Close the distance and strike between beam charges; a bow
works from cover as well. Its spines punish careless contact, so time your
hits instead of spamming.
Useful tips: Water breathing potions and doors or soul sand for air pockets
make monument raids possible; kill the elder guardians first so Mining
Fatigue stops slowing every block you break.

=== Elder Guardian ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: The rare, larger guardian that guards ocean monuments — exactly
three live in each monument.
Where it spawns: Inside ocean monuments, one in each wing plus the central
treasure area.
Behavior: Fires the same charging laser as a guardian and periodically
sends Mining Fatigue to every nearby player, slowing block breaking to a
crawl so you cannot tear into the monument.
Drops: Prismarine crystals and a wet sponge.
How to fight it: Bring a solid sword, clear the small guardians first for
room, then corner each elder against a wall and burst it down before the
next Mining Fatigue wave.
Useful tips: Sponges dry out wet rooms and are the trophy of a monument
raid; kill the three elders before mining anything valuable.

=== Shulker ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A stationary box-like mob native to End cities that fires
homing bullets.
Where it spawns: Clinging to the floors, walls, and ceilings of End cities
and their ships, often in pairs.
Behavior: Stands still until attacked; opens its shell and shoots a guiding
shulker bullet that curves after the player and inflicts Levitation on hit —
floating up uncontrolled into a ceiling is the real danger.
Drops: Shulker shells, used to craft shulker boxes (portable storage that
keeps its contents when moved).
How to fight it: Shoot it from range, or duck behind walls so the homing
bullet crashes into the terrain; never fight one directly under a low
ceiling.
Useful tips: Wear a Slow Falling potion or keep an elytra ready so levitation
never drops you to death; collect several shells for a shulker box set.

=== Warden ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Mob
What it is: A blind, extremely tanky deep-dark guardian that hunts by
vibration — the most dangerous mob in the game.
Where it spawns: In ancient cities deep underground, summoned when players
trigger sculk shriekers.
Behavior: Blind but hypersensitive to sound: footsteps, blocks breaking, and
projectiles all give away your position. It attacks with melee in close
range and with a sonic boom that ignores armor entirely.
Drops: Nothing — the Warden is designed to be avoided, not farmed.
How to fight it: You should not fight it. Sneak everywhere in the deep dark,
walk on wool to muffle vibrations, and avoid breaking blocks. If it
emerges, run toward open tunnels; it can dig after you and kills in one or
two hits.
Useful tips: The sculk sensors around it trigger shriekers — destroy
shriekers before they can summon a second one, and loot ancient cities only
while crouched.

=== Mob Spawning Mechanics ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Game Mechanics
Hostile mobs spawn in light level 0 in recent versions (historically light level 7
or below) on solid blocks away from the player. Spawning is limited to the
128-block range around the player. Placing torches and lighting up caves prevents
spawns.
