# Minecraft — Biomes

The important Overworld, Nether, and End biomes: what each one contains, which
mobs live there, and how to survive in them.

=== How Biomes Work ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
A biome is a region with its own climate, terrain, plants, mobs, and
structures. Biomes decide which trees grow, where villages and temples
generate, which ores are common (emeralds only in mountains, extra gold in
badlands), and which animals spawn. Use an explorer map or wander high and
low — surface changes such as cacti, spruce trees, or coral usually announce a
new biome.

=== Forests ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: The most common biome — oak and birch trees, flowers, and
plentiful wood.
Resources: Wood, apples, and passive mobs; wolves can spawn in forest and
taiga areas.
Mobs: Hostile mobs spawn at night under the canopy, so light up your base.
Why it matters: Forests are the classic starting biome: endless wood, flat
build space nearby, and villages often generate in the neighboring plains.

=== Plains ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: Wide open grasslands with few trees and gentle hills.
Resources: Grass, animals (horses and cows spawn here), and flowers.
Structures: Villages commonly generate in plains, along with the occasional
ruined portal.
Survival: Open sight lines make night travel safer, but there is little cover
from skeletons — keep a shield ready and bed down in a village house.

=== Deserts ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: Hot, treeless sand deserts with cacti and dead bushes.
Resources: Sand for glass and smelting, cactus for green dye, and no wood at
all — bring your own planks.
Structures: Desert temples hide loot chambers under a TNT trap, and desert
villages generate on the sand.
Mobs: Husks (zombies that do not burn in sunlight) replace normal zombies, so
days are not safe here.
Survival: Never break the sand pillar under a desert temple's colored floor
without checking for the TNT and pressure-plate trap beneath it.

=== Jungles ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: Dense, tall jungles with giant trees, vines, and thick canopy.
Resources: Cocoa beans, melons, bamboo, and parrots; jungle temples hide
redstone puzzles and loot.
Mobs: Ocelots and parrots appear here; hostile mobs spawn under the thick
shade.
Survival: Visibility is poor — cut a path or climb above the canopy, and mark
your way with torches so you can find your base again.

=== Taiga and Snowy Biomes ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: Spruce forests, sweet berry bushes, and snowy variants with
frozen rivers and wolves.
Resources: Spruce wood, sweet berries (an easy early food), and snow for
snowballs.
Structures: Villages generate in taigas, and ruined portals dot snowy areas.
Survival: Snow and hills slow travel; carry food and a bed, and watch for
strays (skeleton variants) that fire slowness arrows.

=== Mountains ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: High peaks, steep cliffs, and snow-capped ranges (meadows,
groves, snowy slopes, jagged peaks).
Resources: Emerald ore generates only in mountain biomes, plus generous iron
exposed in the cliffs.
Mobs: Goats roam the peaks and can knock you off ledges.
Survival: Never dig at your feet on a cliff edge, carry a water bucket for
long drops, and build fences around high bases.

=== Swamps ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: Murky, flat wetlands with lily pads, shallow water, and
mushrooms.
Resources: Clay, mushrooms, and slime — slimes spawn in swamps at night and
in swamp chunks underground.
Structures: Witch huts generate in swamps and belong to witches.
Survival: Movement is slow in water; craft a boat, keep weapons ready for
slimes, and light up any hut you claim as a base.

=== Ocean Biomes ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: Oceans range from lukewarm to frozen, with kelp forests,
coral reefs in warm variants, and deep trenches.
Structures: Shipwrecks (with treasure and explorer maps), ocean ruins,
drowned-guarded monuments, and buried treasure on beaches found via maps.
Resources: Kelp, fish, sand, gravel, clay, and prismarine from monuments.
Mobs: Drowned rise from the depths; dolphins and fish swim everywhere.
Survival: Carry a boat and water breathing potions for long dives, and block
yourself into a shipwreck at night rather than swimming in the open.

=== Badlands ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: Layered red, orange, and white terracotta plateaus with mineshafts exposed on the surface.
Resources: Terracotta and hardened clay for building, extra gold ore
throughout, and abandoned mineshafts with wooden supports full of loot.
Mobs: Standard hostile mobs at night; no villages generate here.
Survival: Easy to get lost among the canyons — build landmarks, and bring
plenty of torches when exploring the exposed mineshafts.

=== Nether Biomes ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: The Nether has four main biomes:
- Nether Wastes: Classic hellish terrain with zombified piglins, quartz ore,
  nether gold ore, and fortresses.
- Crimson Forest: Red crimson trees, hoglins, piglins, and fungi; hug the
  fungi lines to keep hoglins away.
- Warped Forest: Blue warped growth that spawns mostly endermen — the safest
  Nether biome to pause in; collect ender pearls here.
- Soul Sand Valley: Soul sand and soul soil with bone-fossil walls; ghasts
  hover over the open caverns and the soul sand itself slows your movement, so
  watch your step and keep cover overhead.
- Basalt Deltas: Jagged basalt and blackstone with magma cubes everywhere and
  lava pockets between nearly every safe step.
Survival: Never sleep, always carry fire resistance, wear gold for piglins,
and build bridges with a wall on one side.

=== The End Biomes ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
What they are: The End splits into the central island (where the dragon
fights) and the outer islands, divided into End Highlands, End Midlands, and
End Barrens.
Resources: Chorus fruit on the outer islands, End cities with enchanted loot,
and end ships holding elytra.
Mobs: Endermen everywhere (neutral unless looked at) and shulkers in the
cities.
Survival: Bridge carefully between islands, wear a pumpkin or avoid eye
contact, and bring firework rockets once you claim an elytra.

=== Choosing a Starting Biome ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Biomes
Which biome should I start in?
- Plains or forest: best all-round start — wood, flat ground, nearby villages.
- Taiga: spruce wood, sweet berries, and villages.
- Ocean shore: shipwrecks and buried treasure for fast early loot, but food is
  scarcer.
- Avoid starting in deserts or badlands until you have tools: no wood and
  tougher mobs make the first night harder.
