# Minecraft — Farming

Crops, animals, mob farms, and automation: how to feed yourself and fuel a
trading hall from the very first day.

=== Farming Mechanics ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
How does farming work? Till dirt or grass blocks with a hoe to create farmland
and keep it within four blocks of water so it stays hydrated (hydrated
farmland is darker and grows crops faster). Crops need light to grow and
cannot be planted on farmland that has trampled back to dirt — jumping or
placing blocks on farmland reverts it. Bone meal fertilizes any crop
instantly. Crops grow through several stages over time; harvesting breaks the
top-growth crop and leaves seeds to replant. Animals can be farmed by penning
two of the same species and feeding them their favorite food to breed them.

=== Wheat ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
How to obtain: Punch tall grass for seeds, till farmland, plant the seeds,
and harvest the golden wheat when fully grown.
Uses: Bread (three wheat in a row = one of the best early foods), cookies,
cakes, and breeding food for cows and sheep.
Practical tips: The first crop every player should plant — a 9x9 farm with a
water source in the middle feeds you indefinitely. Wheat farms also supply
your trading hall: farmers buy wheat for emeralds.

=== Carrots and Potatoes ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
How to obtain: Both are rare zombie drops early on and generate in village
farm plots; carrots plant directly (no seed stage) and potatoes likewise.
Uses: Carrots breed pigs, craft golden carrots (the longest night-vision and
strong horse food), and are eaten raw; potatoes can be eaten raw or baked in
a furnace for baked potatoes — one of the cheapest high-saturation foods.
Practical tips: Plant a full row of each as soon as you find a village;
they multiply quickly with bone meal and need no seed stage.

=== Beetroot ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
How to obtain: Beetroot seeds come from beetroot crops found in village farms
and from shipwreck chests; plant seeds, harvest beetroot.
Uses: Beetroot soup (a filling bowl meal), red dye, and beetroot seeds are
chicken breeding food.
Practical tips: A minor food crop, but farmers buy beetroot for emeralds and
it stacks with your other village crop trades.

=== Sugar Cane ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
How to obtain: Plant sugar cane on sand, dirt, or grass directly next to water;
it grows up to three blocks tall and can be planted on the block above water
too.
Uses: Sugar for speed potions and cookies, and paper — paper makes bookshelves
(for your enchanting setup), maps, and fireworks rockets (elytra fuel).
Practical tips: Plant long single rows along your pond's edge and harvest the
bottom two blocks, leaving the base to regrow automatically.

=== Bamboo ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
How to obtain: Found growing in jungles (and from wandering trader or shipwreck
chests); plant it on dirt, grass, sand, or moss and it spreads fast.
Uses: Scaffolding for building, smelting fuel (inefficient but abundant), and
decorative sticks; pandas eat it.
Practical tips: Bamboo grows faster than any other plant — a small jungle
patch becomes a renewable scaffolding and fuel farm with almost no effort.

=== Animals and Breeding ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
How do I farm animals? Pen two of the same animal, feed each its favorite
food, and a baby spawns after a short delay with a small experience bonus:
- Cows and sheep: wheat
- Pigs: carrots, potatoes, or beetroots
- Chickens: any seeds
- Wolves: any meat
- Cats: raw fish
- Horses: golden apples or golden carrots
Baby animals grow into adults in minutes and can be bred again after a short
cooldown. Keep one male and female of each species in labeled pens and breed
them on a schedule — cooked meat, leather, feathers, and wool then never run
out.

=== Mob Farms ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
What is a mob farm? Any build that collects mob drops automatically. The
simplest designs are:
- Spawner farm: find a dungeon spawner (zombie or skeleton), clear a room
  around it, funnel mobs into a water channel, and drop them to low health so
  you can finish them for XP and loot.
- Dark-room farm: an unlit platform high in the sky where mobs spawn, with
  water streams pushing them to a central chute.
- Enderman or other XP farms: built after the End for massive experience.
Safety: Light up surrounding caves so spawns concentrate in your farm, use
hoppers under collection points to fill chests, and stand within 16 blocks of
the spawner to keep it active.

=== Basic Automated Farms ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
How do I automate a farm?
- Collection: run water streams to a central point where a hopper feeds a
  chest; hopper minecarts under a large field pick up items through blocks.
- Harvesting: a dispenser with a water bucket flushes grown crops when
  triggered; pistons can smash melons and pumpkins.
- Detection: observers watch a fully grown crop and fire the harvest pulse.
- Convenience: auto-smelters use a hopper to feed a furnace and another to
  pull food out; fuel can be fed from above.
Start simple — a water-flush wheat or carrot field with hopper collection
already gives passive food, then add observers and redstone as your redstone
supply grows.

=== Practical Farming Tips ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Farming
- Water hydrates farmland up to four blocks in every direction — one water
  block can cover a 9x9 plot.
- Light farms with torches so crops grow at night and mobs cannot spawn in
  your rows.
- Fence animal pens so they cannot trample crops or escape; gates make
  entrances.
- Keep a composter next to the farm: excess crops become bone meal, which
  grows the next batch instantly.
- Farmers restock their trade twice a day — feed your trading-hall farmer and
  harvest for a steady emerald income.
- Never build farms directly under trees: leaf shadows and falling saplings
  make a mess.
