# Minecraft — Redstone

Redstone from the first lever to working machines: components, mechanics, and
practical builds you can copy.

=== Redstone Basics ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
How does redstone work? Redstone dust carries a power signal up to 15 blocks
from a power source such as a lever, button, redstone torch, or pressure
plate. A signal loses one strength per block of dust, so it dies after 15;
repeaters refresh it back to full. Powered dust glows bright red; unpowered
dust is dark. Redstone components only work when they receive power: pistons
extend, lamps light, dispensers fire, and doors open.

=== Redstone Dust ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
What it is: The wire of Minecraft — crafted from four redstone dust drops
(mine redstone ore with an iron pickaxe below y 16).
How to use: Place it on top of solid, transparent-ok blocks to connect
components; it climbs one block at a time like a staircase and cannot climb a
full wall. Dust powers the block it sits on and the component next to it.
Tips: Silk Touch mines redstone ore for placement elsewhere; a single redstone
line between a lever and a door is the simplest circuit in the game.

=== Levers and Buttons ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
- Lever: a permanent on/off toggle — flip it to power the line until flipped
  back. Best for doors, lamps, and pistons you want to hold a state.
- Buttons: a short pulse that turns off by itself (stone and wooden buttons
  differ slightly in length; wooden buttons can be triggered by arrows).
- Pressure plates: wooden plates trigger under items and mobs; stone plates
  only under players and living mobs — perfect for secret doors and traps.
Tips: Levers remember state, buttons do not — use a lever for machines you
toggle and buttons for gates you pass through.

=== Pistons and Sticky Pistons ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
Materials: Piston = 3 planks + 4 cobblestone + 1 iron ingot + 1 redstone
dust; sticky piston = piston + 1 slimeball.
How they work: A powered piston pushes up to 12 blocks (and entities) one
block forward. Sticky pistons push and then pull the block back when power
releases.
Tips: Slimeballs come from slimes (swamps and deep slime chunks), so start a
slime farm if you build redstone heavily. Use pistons for hidden doors and
automatic harvesting — but pistons cannot move chests, furnaces, or other
container blocks.

=== Observers ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
What it does: An observer watches the block it faces and emits a short
redstone pulse whenever that block changes — crops growing, a door opening,
a furnace finishing, or any block update.
How to craft: Built from cobblestone, redstone dust, and nether quartz in a
crafting table (the recipe book shows the exact pattern with the 'face'
on the output side).
Tips: Observers are the heart of automatic farms: one watches the crop, the
pulse fires a dispenser of water or a piston, and the harvest flushes into
your hoppers.

=== Redstone Repeaters ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
How they work: A repeater takes a weak signal and outputs full strength —
extending a line another 15 blocks — and adds a delay of one to four redstone
ticks that you set by clicking it. Repeaters are directional: they only
forward a signal one way, which also makes them useful as diodes and as
long-delay timing elements.
Recipe: Stone, redstone torches, and redstone dust in a crafting table.
Tips: Space repeaters every 15 blocks for long wires, chain them for precise
timing in doors and item sorters, and remember a repeater powers the block in
front of it.

=== Redstone Torches ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
How it works: A redstone torch powers a line by default and turns OFF when
the block it is attached to receives power — this inversion makes it a NOT
gate, the basis of logic circuits. Torch towers carry signals vertically
upward (each torch powers the block above, which inverts the next torch).
Tips: Torches also burn out if toggled too fast, which is the classic
'stuck-on' bug in unstable clocks.

=== Comparators ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
What it does: A comparator reads the contents of a container (a chest, hopper,
furnace, brewing stand, or shulker box) and outputs a redstone signal
proportional to how full it is — the fuller the container, the stronger the
signal. In compare mode it passes a signal only if it is stronger than the
side input; flip it to subtract mode to subtract the side signal.
Recipe: Nether quartz, stone, and three redstone torches.
Tips: Comparators are how automatic sorters and full-container alarms work:
a hopper behind a comparator with a redstone lock turns any chest into a
filtered storage system.

=== Hoppers ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
Materials: 5 iron ingots + 1 chest.
Recipe: Five iron ingots in a V shape with the chest in the bottom-center
slot.
How they work: A hopper pulls items from a container above it and pushes
items into the container it points at (its spout aims down by default —
rotate it with sneak-click against the target). Hoppers move one item at a
time every short interval and lock when powered by redstone.
Tips: Hoppers under farms collect drops automatically; a hopper feeding a
furnace and another pulling the output makes an automatic smelter.

=== Basic Redstone Machines ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
What are good first redstone builds?
- Automatic door: two sticky pistons on each side of a hallway wired to
  pressure plates — step on the plate and the blocks retract.
- Lever-controlled lighting: a lever through a wall to a redstone lamp
  circuit lights a room without torches.
- Automatic farm harvest: observer watches a crop, pulses a dispenser with a
  water bucket, and water flushes everything into a hopper chest.
- Auto-smelter: chests on top of and beside furnaces with hoppers feeding
  fuel and ore in and pulling items out.
- Simple item sorter: a hopper locked by a comparator lets only matching
  items pass into its chest — the base of any storage room.
- Redstone clock: two repeaters in a loop with a torch create a repeating
  pulse that drives flashing lights or farm timers.

=== Practical Redstone Tips ===
Game: Minecraft
Category: Redstone
- Plan on a grid: redstone dust on the floor is easy to misread — mark lines
  with wool under them.
- Repeaters refresh signals and add delay; comparators measure containers;
  torches invert — those three components solve most problems.
- Power the block a component sits on (a lever on the wall powers the block
  behind the dust below it).
- Isolate circuits: a line crossing another carries power into it — route
  with slabs or step overs.
- Test small: build one cell of a machine first, then tile it.
- Bring glowstone or redstone lamps so you can see signal strength while
  testing — powered dust glows visibly.
